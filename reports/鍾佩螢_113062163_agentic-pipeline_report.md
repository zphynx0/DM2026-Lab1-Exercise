# Your report

The dataset contains 2,257 documents from four newsgroup categories (`load_dataset_1`). Initial inspection confirmed 3 columns: text, category, and category name (`inspect_data_2`). No missing values or duplicate records were found (`check_missing_3`, `check_duplicates_4`). Document lengths ranged from 125 to 60,713 characters, with a median of 1,235 (`describe_data_5`). A random sample of 500 documents was also generated without modifying the original dataset (`sample_data_6`).

The document-term matrix contained 35,788 terms and had 99.547% sparsity (`dtm_7`), which is normal because each document contains only a small portion of the vocabulary. Term frequency analysis showed that common words such as “the,” “of,” and “to” appeared most often (`tf_8`). The heatmap showed that most entries in the selected matrix slice were 0 (`dtm_heatmap_9`). Variance filtering reduced the vocabulary to 709 terms (`variance_filter_10`), while Pearson filtering for `comp.graphics` kept 12 terms at a threshold of 0.2, including “vga,” “video,” and “card” (`pearson_filter_11`). The feature correlation matrix showed strong correlations between common words, such as “the” and “of” (0.9442) (`feature_corr_12`).

Pattern mining on `comp.graphics` used variance filtering, FP-Growth, and a min-sup of 20 (`patterns_13`). The most frequent patterns included “organization” (support=560), “newsreader” (58), and “tin” (56). Some multi-word patterns, such as “newsreader organization” (53), were also found. Unlike Pearson filtering, which finds words related to a category, FP-Growth finds words or word combinations that appear often together. Many patterns were common words or email-related terms, so frequent occurrence didn't necessarily mean strong topic-specific relevance.

However, the 4 categories overlapped in the plot, so they weren't clearly separated. Label binarization converted the 4 categories into a 2,257 × 4 matrix (`binarize_labels_15`). Cosine similarity was 0.3532 for documents 0 and 1, both from `comp.graphics`, and 0.3399 for documents 0 and 2, from `comp.graphics` and `soc.religion.christian`, from different categories. The small difference shows that documents from the same category were slightly more similar in their term-frequency patterns.
