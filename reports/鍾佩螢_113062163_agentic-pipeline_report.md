# Your report

The dataset contains 2,257 documents from four newsgroup categories: alt.atheism, comp.graphics, sci.med, and soc.religion.christian (`load_dataset_1`). Inspecting rows, checking for missing text, checking for duplicates, and describing document lengths all came back clean, so no rows were removed. The document-term matrix (`dtm_3`) contains 35,788 terms and is 99.547% sparse, meaning most documents contain only a small portion of the vocabulary.

Term frequency analysis (`tf_7`) showed that common words such as “the,” “of,” and “to” appeared most frequently. The variance filter (`variance_filter_8`) reduced the vocabulary from 35,788 to 709 terms, while the Pearson filter (`pearson_filter_9`, target=comp.graphics, threshold=0.2) identified 12 terms correlated with the comp.graphics category. Positive terms included “vga,” “video,” and “card,” which relate to computer graphics, while "re," "writes," and "people" were negatively correlated. However, the correlations were weak (the strongest was 0.28), suggesting that individual words alone cannot clearly distinguish this category. The correlation heatmap (`feature_corr_10`) also showed strong relationships between common words, such as "the" and "of" (0.944), possibly because longer documents contain more words overall.

Pattern mining on the comp.graphics category using FP-Growth with TF-IDF filtering and a minimum support of 10 ran out of memory. Therefore, the filtering method was changed to term frequency. At a minimum support of 30 (patterns_11), no patterns were found. Lowering the threshold to 20 (`patterns_12`) identified seven single-word patterns: “recently” and “personally” (22 documents each), “rumours,” “wondering,” and “long” (21 each), and “hard” and “26” (20 each). Support represents the number of documents containing a pattern. No multi-word patterns met the threshold, and the identified words were not particularly specific to computer graphics.

PCA reduced the document-term matrix (`dtm_3`, 2,257 × 35,788) to two dimensions (`reduce_dimensions_4`). PC1 explains 65.94% of the variance and PC2 explains 4.33%, accounting for 70.27% combined. Since the matrix uses raw word counts without stop-word removal, PC1 may largely reflect document length and frequent words rather than topic. The plot shows the distribution of the four categories, with points colored by category. In the plot, the 4 categories overlap heavily in a dense cluster near the origin, so PCA doesn't separate them by topic. A few outlier documents with very large values on 1 or both axes stretch the scale, probably a long documents with large word counts, which supports the idea that PC1 mainly reflects document length and frequent words.

The cosine similarity between documents 0 and 1 (`cosine_sim_6`) was 0.3532, a moderate overlap. Because the vectors are raw counts that include stop words, common words contribute to this score.
